\#\#\# How does Corporate America Hire?

When a company has an open position and they need another person to join, they  
give a manager budget for a new hire. This manager is called a Hiring Manager  
(HM).

This HM is just a regular manager who needs to hire. It is rarely their whole job  
and most HMs don’t have a lot of experience in hiring, some may only hire once  
every few years.

To help with this process as HMs don’t usually have the time to handle all of the  
issues in hiring, they engage Recruiters to help them manage the companies open  
positions.

\#\#\# What is the corporate hiring process

The steps to hire a candidate, which is what Recruiters call people that are looking for a  
job are:

1\. HM gets budget for a new role  
2\. HM engages a Recruiter  
3\. Recruiter gathers the qualifications (keywords) for the role from the HM  
4\. Recruiter posts a job posting to a job board (Indeed, LinkedIn, Monster, etc)  
5\. Recruiter reviews resumes candidates send for the qualifications the HM wants  
6\. Those resumes pass a pre-screen (optional) and get sent to the HM  
7\. The HM interviews candidates  
8\. An Offer is made to the best candidate or if no candidates meet the HMS criteria, as  
    an HM would rather no hire than a “bad hire” the process restarts at step 5\#

\#\#\# How to tell if your resume is “good”

A good rule of thumb to tell if a resume is good by how many interviews you get vs  
the number of applications you send. You can give them grades based on the  
following ratios.

1 to 10 \= A+

\#\#\# What a resume is and isn’t

Resume are:

\`\`\`  
● Made to showcase that you meet the minimum qualifications for the role  
● Tailored to a specific job title  
\`\`\`  
They are terrible at:

\`\`\`  
● Showing Brags  
● Marketing  
● Graphic Design  
\`\`\`  
The only goal of your resume is to show you meet the minimum qualifications for  
the job title you are applying to.

\#\#\# The most important step in resume creation

Before writing your first words on your resume, you need to find the keywords for  
your job title.

What are keywords? Keywords are shorthand for Qualifications that both the  
Recruiter and HM look for to determine if you meet the minimum qualifications for  
the role.

Both Recruiter and HM have 15 seconds to look at your resume, and they need to  
see HOW you used those keywords and WHY you used them in those 15 seconds  
or else they will reject you.

\#\#\# What is a Job Title?

Job titles are the common name for a certain set of qualifications that HM are  
looking for, as an example a HM/Recruiter will look for the following in a resume  
for each of these job titles

\`\`\`  
● Barista: POS, Customer Service, Basic Math, Multitasking  
● Accountant: Excel, Generally Accepted Accounting Principles, GAAP, CPA  
● IT Helpdesk: Customer Service, Troubleshooting, MS Office, Zoom, Windows  
● Project Manager: PMP/CSM, Client/Stakeholders, Agile, Cross Functional  
\`\`\`

\#\#\# What is a Job Description (JD)

JDs contain all the information about the position such as but not limited to

\`\`\`  
● Job Title  
● Salary  
● Day to Day duties  
● EEOC/EOE Statements  
\`\`\`  
You can ignore all of the above, as that is not relevant for your resume. The only  
thing in the JD that matters is the Qualifications.

\#\#\# What are Qualifications

Keywords are found in the following sections in bullet points

\`\`\`  
● Requirements  
● Qualifications  
● Must Have  
● Need to Have  
● What we are Looking for  
● We Would Love To Meet You If  
● You Might Be a Great Fit If  
\`\`\`  
Everything under that section in a JD under a bullet point is a Qualification. These are the only  
thing that matter in constructing your resume.

Not all JDs are good, some are terrible and don’t include the keywords. If a JD does not include  
the qualifications it is a Bad JDs. If it contains those keywords in those sections it is a Good JD.

\#\#\# How to find the right Qualifications

1\. Go to Indeed, LinkedIn, Monster, etc to find jobs  
2\. Find a Good JD that you meet 75% of the qualifications for and copy the  
    keywords down on a separate sheet of paper/Excel Sheet.  
3\. Repeat this process 10 to 15 more times for JDs that have the same job title.  
4\. Mark how many times each of those qualifications appeared in those JDs  
5\. That is the list of qualifications you are going to use to create your resume.  
    The more they appeared, the more important they are.

For each different job title you want to apply to, you will need to do the above.  
Different job titles have different qualifications and resumes are job title specific.

An amazing resume that gets you a Project Manager job, will fail to get you a  
Barista job and vice versa.

\#\#\# Deciding Which of the Two Resumes You Need

There are two types of resumes: Reverse Chronological Resume and Three Skill  
Bucket Resume. Neither is better than the other, as your work history will  
determine which is best for you. You don’t need to learn them both, only the one  
that is best suited to you based on your work history.

WARNING: Never combine the two formats, into one super format. They don’t  
work well together. If you combine them it will fail.

\`\`\`  
● Italicize your your job title, company, state, (left centered) and years of

● Projects do not need to include months/years

\#\#\# Job Summary

● Create a summary sentence of what you did at this job, this sentence needs  
to include a few keywords from our keyword list and needs to be basic  
enough that someone with no industry knowledge could understand  
● This sentence needs to be in a bullet point  
● This sentence can be 3 lines maximum and should not include more than 1  
period  
● This sentence is to be written in the past tense, even if you are still employed

\#\#\# Past Experience pt 1: What, How, Result/Reason

\`\`\`  
● Create a bullet point that includes keywords from your keyword list, the more the better  
● The sentence needs to show HOW you used the keyword and the result or reason you used the  
keyword  
● Often the What, How, Result/Reason will overlap and that is ok.  
● This sentence needs to be in a bullet point  
● This sentence can be 3 lines maximum and should not include more than 1 period  
● This sentence is to be written in the past tense even if you are still employed  
\`\`\`

\#\#\# Past Experience pt 2: Example of What, How, Result/Reason

\`\`\`  
● Sentence 1:  
○ What: Customer Service, Multitasking  
○ How: Handled a large lunch line by performing customer service and multitasking  
○ Result: 5 star google reviews  
● Sentence 2:  
○ What: Perform Basic Math  
○ How: Added and Subtracted Cash  
○ Reason: Gave customers correct change  
\`\`\`

\#\#\# Past Experience pt 3: Repeat

\`\`\`  
● Once you have created your bullet point with keywords in a What, How,  
Reason/Result format you will need to do it again  
● Create a new sentence involving different keywords  
● Repeat this process until you have a minimum of 3 bullet points (including  
Summary) or a maximum of 8 Bullet Points (including the summary)  
depending on how long you where at that job  
● Once you reach 3 to 8 total bullet points ( 1 summary sentence and 2 to 7  
What, How, Result/Reason sentences) you are finished with this job  
\`\`\`

\#\#\# Repeat, Repeat, Repeat

\`\`\`  
● Move on to the next job repeating the Job Information, Job Summary, Past  
Experience  
● You want to put no more than 12 years of experience in this manner (with the  
exception of High Level Directors/Managers)  
● Your goal should be to have 75% of the keywords in your keyword list in the  
first half of the first page of your resume  
● Once you have finished you are good to send your resume out  
\`\`\`

\#\#\# What is an ATS?

\`\`\`  
● Recruiters have between 35 to 70 open positions at the same time  
● Recruiters use Applicant Tracking Systems to keep track of all of our open  
requisitions (Open Jobs), see where candidates are in the process (first  
interview, second interview, offer, or rejected), and a bunch of other Recruiter  
specific needs  
● There are a lot of rumors of what an ATS does, how it hurts candidates  
chances of getting a job, and AI Scanning technology  
● Don’t believe those rumors  
\`\`\`

\#\#\# Why is Faster Better

\`\`\`  
● ATS sorts people in the order they applied. Anything that increases your time  
to hit "submit" in the ATS will lower your chances.  
● If you are resume \#139, the recruiter may find who they need at number \#75,  
and once we fill up ours/managers' schedule with interviews, we stop looking  
unless the HM needs more candidates.  
● Yes, AI ATS and Resume Scanning do exist, but they exist in such small  
numbers that unless you are specifically applying for an AI company, you will  
only see an AI ATS in 1 out of 100 applications.  
● The default setting for the majority of ATS on the market (Such as Workday,  
ADP, and Taleo) is first-come, first-served  
● Thus the faster you apply the better your chances  
\`\`\`
